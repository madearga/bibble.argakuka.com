The build is unsigned, so the first launch asks for one confirmation: right-click the app and choose Open. That is the whole ceremony. Seven public-domain translations ride inside the binary, so it has scripture on a Mac that never installed anything.

Things you might be wondering.

Why does the reading feel different?
The text is set in Literata at a patient measure, with poetry indented by its levels, the divine name in small capitals, and the words of Christ in red when you want them. Verse numbers are superscripts in the margin, the way a printed reader's Bible does it. You can read verse per line or in flowing paragraphs, and every setting is remembered.
What do the study tools actually do?
Six highlight colours, and the wash is painted behind the verse's own words — including the words it shares a line with in a paragraph. Notes, bookmarks, and a study list in the sidebar that shows a snippet of every marked verse. Everything is keyed to a canonical grid of 31,102 verses, so your marks follow the verse when you switch translations.
Can I bring my own translation?
Yes. The app imports USFM, OSIS, Zefania, and SWORD modules — locked SWORD modules included, by passphrase. Seven public-domain translations are already compiled in: KJV, WEB, BSB, ASV, YLT, Darby, and Webster.
Is there really no network code?
There is no network code. The app makes no request at any point — everything it needs is compiled in or sits in your data folder. Your study data is one SQLite file next to your translations, and it exports to Markdown or JSON whenever you want it out.
Is it fast?
Whole-Bible search runs in about half a millisecond, a full chapter decodes in five, and scrolling costs the app about twelve microseconds per frame. Those are measured budgets from the project's own benchmarks, not marketing numbers, and every release is checked against them.
What is the clean-room story?
The design was allowed to study how another reader solves its problems; the source was written from scratch for this repository and says so in every file's header. THIRD_PARTY.md is precise about what that means, because overclaiming is how these stories go wrong.

Questions? Answers.

Does it phone home?

It doesn't own a phone.

Why is the app icon a single letter?

Because the letter is set in the same face as the scripture, and the accent rule under it is the same colour as the margin marks. Restraint is the design.
